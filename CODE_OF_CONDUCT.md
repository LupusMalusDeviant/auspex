# Code of conduct

## Short

Treat people so that they come back gladly. Criticism is aimed at the code,
not the person. Somebody asking a question that is trivial to you is asking
it for the first time.

## Expected

- Stay factual, even when the other side does not.
- Assume a contribution comes in good faith — an unsuitable one included.
- Give reasons with criticism. "That is wrong" helps nobody; "that breaks on
  IPv6 addresses with a zone index" does.
- Admit a mistake when it shows, and carry on.

## Not acceptable

- Insults, belittling, allusions to origin, gender, appearance, religion,
  disability, age or sexual orientation.
- Sexualised language or imagery, advances of any kind.
- Personal attacks, insisting on an argument after a decision.
- Publishing other people's private data without their explicit permission.

This holds in issues, pull requests, commit messages and anywhere else
somebody speaks for this project.

## If something happens

Report it through [private vulnerability reporting][pvr] in the repository
or, if that does not fit, by direct message to the maintainers on GitHub.
What is reported stays confidential.

The maintainers may edit or remove comments, reject contributions and, in
clear cases, block access. They explain their decision when asked.

[pvr]: https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/security-advisories/guidance-on-reporting-and-writing-information-about-vulnerabilities/privately-reporting-a-security-vulnerability
